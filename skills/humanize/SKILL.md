# Humanize

**AI tells** are the habits that make readers think "a chatbot wrote this". People discount the message, and the person who sent it, once they spot them.

## Process

1. Write from the specifics the user gave: names, numbers, dates, the actual reason. Where a detail that matters is missing, put a short `[placeholder]`. Never invent experiences, feelings, quotes or anecdotes for the user. Done when every sentence is either the user's own fact or a placeholder.
2. Check the draft against every tell below and rewrite each hit, then scan once more for contrasts ("not just", "not X but Y") and dashes, which survive a first pass. Done when none are left.

   <tells>
   - Punctuation: em dashes (use a comma, a period or parentheses); emoji as bullets or decoration; bold phrases inside short messages; more than two hashtags.
   - Words: delve, tapestry, testament, landscape, realm, journey, chapter, navigate (anything but routes), embark, foster, leverage, elevate, empower, unlock, seamless, robust, vibrant, pivotal, crucial, invaluable, game-changer, thrilled, excited to announce, bittersweet, humbled.
   - Shapes: "not just X, it's Y" and "it's not X, it's Y"; lists of three added for rhythm; "from X to Y" ranges; a question the writer answers next ("The result?"); "Here's the thing:"; sentences ending in "…, highlighting/underscoring/reflecting the importance of…"; stacks of one-line paragraphs; every paragraph the same length.
   - Openers and closers: "I hope this finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", "Great question", "In summary", "Ultimately", "Feel free to reach out", "Let me know if you have any questions", "Looking forward to a swift resolution", a closing question asking the audience to engage.
   - Content: praise with no example; claims of significance ("a pivotal moment"); balanced hedging when the user has an opinion; restating the same point to end.
   </tells>

3. Make it shorter than your first draft. Plain verbs, contractions, sentences of different lengths, and the tone the user would use in person with this reader. Done when the user could say every line out loud to the recipient without wincing.

Return only the finished text. Add notes after it only when the user asked for them or a `[placeholder]` needs filling.
