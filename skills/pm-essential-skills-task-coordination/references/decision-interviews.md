# Pressure-test a plan or prepare an asynchronous questionnaire

Use when asked to challenge a plan, decision or idea (including “grill me”), or
when an important unresolved choice needs a focused interview. An explicit
challenge request applies even to a previously accepted plan. It authorizes
examining the plan, not silently replacing accepted decisions or starting work.
Clear execution requests need no interview by default.

## Challenge with concrete consequences

Recover the intended outcome, settled choices, constraints and available facts.
Identify assumptions whose failure would change feasibility, scope, cost, timing
or acceptance. Try a concrete counterexample or competing explanation; do not
invent objections merely to sound adversarial. Compare the proposed plan with
the actual sources. Separate observed contradictions, uncertain facts and
choices belonging to a person. Look up accessible facts rather than asking the
user to repeat them. Use the relevant domain method when needed and available;
this procedure does not supply software, legal or financial expertise.

Treat dependent decisions as a small graph in current context: a question is
ready only when its prerequisites are sufficiently settled. Ask the most useful
ready question, or a small group of independent ones. Honor a requested pace,
including one question at a time. Do not send the whole future questionnaire to
the user just because it can be imagined. Research can proceed alongside
independent questions; delegation is optional and subject to host authority.

For concrete alternatives use permitted native selectable input. Include the
consequence and a recommendation where evidence supports one. Keep open facts
open-ended; make disagreement easy. Do not phrase every question to elicit “yes”
or invent a confidence percentage. Wait for answers before dependent questions
or actions, carrying partial and settled answers forward. Explicit bounded
delegation lets the agent decide within those bounds; silence does not.

After an answer, revise only the affected choices and dependencies. Finish when
the consequential in-scope branches are answered, investigated, explicitly
delegated or deferred with a reason and latest safe decision point. If the user
ends the interview, preserve unresolved consequences without continuing to
interrogate them. Summarize settled decisions, remaining uncertainty and the
next authorized action in the existing artifact/context. No exhaustive tree,
fixed number of rounds, separate tracker or ritual final approval is required.

## Ask the person who actually has the information

For a requested questionnaire, use the known recipient, their knowledge and the
decision the answers will inform. Ask the requester only for material missing
framing they can know; do not make them answer the very questions being sent out.
Draft a self-contained artifact with brief context, known constraints, priority
questions and a usable response space. Each question should seek one answer;
use explicit conditional follow-ups when the questionnaire must work without a
live interviewer. Cover every requested decision, allow uncertainty/partial
answers, and identify needed supporting evidence where it matters. Include a
deadline or response effort only if known or labeled proposed.

Drafting does not authorize sending, contacting recipients, booking, purchasing
or creating shared records. Keep the requested format and destination. On return,
reconcile answers against the original decision and identify consequential
missing/conflicting responses instead of treating a filled form as approval.
